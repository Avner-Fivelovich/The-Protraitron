Updates: Movement & Page Control

servoL vs moveL

Reviewed Movement Modes

To maintain absolute control while moving on the page surface, we thoroughly reviewed the core UR5e control behaviors:

moveL (Linear Position Control) Position control receives higher priority than force mode control. Highly predictable but less compliant with minor paper surface warps.
servoL (Real-Time Control) Allows you to inject positions dynamically in real-time. We discovered that force mode control works best when combined with real-time servo loops!

Challenges: The Infamous Ink Stain

The Lingering Marker Problem

While validating that the pen has reached the page (filtering out high force values caused by noisy sensor readings), the marker lingers at the starting coordinate for the contact validation period.

"Behold! The Unintentional Blot-Inator! It stains the paper before the drawing even begins!"

Our Solution Path

We want to find a clean way to get rid of this initial dwell time blot.

  • Change our drawing tool to a less bleed-heavy medium (e.g. ballpoint, pencil, or spring-loaded tip).
  • We have the ability to calculate the touch plane using empirical numbers. Using this, we can reduce the error space of the force sensor to be 0.5 millimeters away from the drawing plane.

Feasibility & Future Plans

What We Learned

The application is highly feasible! The UR5e arm is precise enough to draw complex high-definition lines on paper.

However, noisy force feedback and physical drawing tool behavior (like initial bleed) require careful control loop optimization to ensure a clean visual finish.

Did Our Plans Change?

NOPE!

We still fully intend to do everything we originally planned! All milestones, from SwiftSketch integration to full face vector drawing, remain on track.

What we don't know yet:

We haven't begun working on camera integration, page handing, and tool grabbing and dropping.
