The PORTRAITRON 3000

Final Presentation

Avner Fivelovich, Hila Regev Shabath, Shira Shmuely

Original Problem & Plan

Functionality

Capture a person standing in a very specific spot! The portrait was to be drawn on paper using our patented marker mount, struck with a commemorative stamp of our own faces, and finally handed over to the subject directly by the robot.

Software & Algorithms

  • Capture: Take a high-definition picture of the subject (3s after a keyboard click).
  • Process: Convert camera input into vector strokes using the SwiftSketch algorithm.
  • Optimize: Run a stroke-order choosing algorithm to make the robot draw efficiently.

Robotics & Execution

  • The Drawing: Pick up the marker contraption, execute the strokes on page (POC goal), and let go.
  • The Stamping: Pick up the stamp, perform stamping motion on the page, and let go.
  • The Handoff (Nice to have): Grab the finished page and physically hand it to a designated location.

What Changed?

Scrapped & Replaced

  • The Algorithm: We realized the off-the-shelf SwiftSketch algorithm wasn't accurate enough for human faces, so we decided to train a custom model ourselves.
  • Direct Handoff & Cutting: We gave up on the robot handing the page directly to the user (using grippers), and we also scrapped the automatic cutting motion with a knife. Instead, the robot gently pulls the paper forward to present it.
  • The Stamp: We gave up on the commemorative stamp with our faces and decided to sign the drawing a bit differently.

Major Additions

  • Web-Based UI: Added an interactive web app so users can take or upload a photo and approve a portrait preview before the physical drawing begins.
  • Paper Roll & Magnets: Replaced manually inserted sheets with a continuous paper roll. The robot pulls a section, holds it tight using top and bottom magnets, draws on it, disconnects them, and after a manual cut, it is handed to the user.

The Final Product: The Portraitron

  • Pulls the paper and straightens it with two magnets (top/bottom).
  • Draws the picture.
  • Disconnects the magnets.
  • Gently pulls the paper forward, allowing a manual cut to hand it to the user.
  • Tool Switching: Picks up and returns the stamp and marker seamlessly.

Work Process & Challenges

  • Tool Mounts: The first pen mount did not fit the drawing tool!
  • The Uneven Wall: Needed enough force to draw, but not too much to explode the marker.
  • Force Mode vs MoveL/MoveJ: We used MoveL/MoveJ for moving, but Force Mode is a PC loop that gets input and defines new locations.
  • Force Mode Misunderstanding: Noise and misunderstanding caused unexpected behavior — like punching a hole in the wall!
  • Calibration: Connecting and moving all the small tools required constant recalibration.
